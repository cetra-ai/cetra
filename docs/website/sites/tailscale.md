- **Subhead:** "A Zero Trust identity-based connectivity platform that replaces your legacy VPN, SASE, and PAM…" It continues with a long list (remote teams, multi-cloud, CI/CD, Edge & IoT, AI workloads).
- **Primary CTA:** "Start connecting devices" (small black button). The nav repeats it as "Get started - it's free!" (black).
- **Secondary CTA:** "Contact sales" (underlined text link).
- **Above the nav:** a mono utility bar (VISIT US AT REINVENT · BLOG · DOCS · DOWNLOAD · CONTACT SALES).
- **Hero visual:** no image above the fold. A row of five product tabs (Business VPN [active, brick red #C64A40], Privileged Access, Securing AI, Infra access, Zero Trust networking) drives a white card below. That card holds a "PRODUCT / Business VPN" pitch, a checklist, and a laptop mockup of the real admin and email UI with a floating "Tailscale Status" panel (Connection Online, Latency 23ms…).

## Structure
1. Utility bar, mega-menu nav (Platform, Solutions, Customers, Community, Partnerships, Pricing), Login and Get started.
2. Centred hero: headline, subhead, CTA pair.
3. Product tab row, then the product card (checklist, "Connect remote teams" button, device mockup with a status widget).
4. "50,000 businesses choose Tailscale": three case-study stat cards (Corelight 1,000+ hours saved, Cribl 25x headcount growth without IT, Instacart 90% fewer support requests), then a 17-logo grid (Cohere, Duolingo, Mistral AI, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Cursor, Perplexity, Revolut…).
5. Blue (#557AD3) band, "Easy, secure, identity-based access to anything": audience chips (IT / Security / DevOps / Engineering) above a composite of a real terminal (`curl -fsSL https://tailscale.com/install.sh | sh`, `sudo tailscale up`, a login URL), the browser sign-in page and the Machines admin table. Then three points: "Installation takes minutes", "Switching is easy", "Bridge hybrid environments".
6. "Developer approved, But don't just take our word for it.": a horizontally scrolling carousel of real X/Twitter posts with handles.
7. "Over 100 integrations": a big icon wall (about 90 tiles) with a centred text card and "See all integrations".
8. Dark rounded closing CTA ("Secure your networking using identity with Tailscale", Get started / Contact sales), then the footer.

## Proof
- "50,000 businesses" as an h2, right after the product card (about 1 screen down).
- Three quantified case studies, each linking to "Read case study".
- A dense logo grid with AI-era names (Mistral, NVIDIA, Cursor, Perplexity, Cohere).
- A wall of developer tweets: authentic, specific and funny ("busts double nat", "installed it on our robots").
- The integration wall implies ecosystem maturity.
- No GitHub stars or benchmarks shown, though the footer links "Open Source".

## Product shown how
- Real UI mockups: the admin Machines table, the sign-in page, a status widget and an email client on a laptop. The first appears at the bottom of the first viewport.
- The install flow is shown as literal terminal commands inside a composite. It is the clearest "how easy is it" moment on the page.
- No video and no canvas (one iframe).

## Visual style
- **Type:** Inter throughout. The h1 is 64px at weight 500 (not bold), -0.64px tracking, which gives a calm, confident tone. Mono for the utility bar and eyebrows.
- **Colours:** warm off-white #F9F7F6, white cards, near-black #1F1F1F buttons and bars, brick red #C64A40 (active tab and "PRODUCT" eyebrow), periwinkle blue #557AD3 (the install band), plus small coloured product icons (purple, blue, green).
- **Mode and density:** light, airy, rounded cards with large radii, medium density.
- **Signature devices:** the 9-dot logo motif, two-tone headlines ("Developer approved," in grey and "But don't just take our word for it." in black; "50,000 businesses" in grey and "choose Tailscale" in black), a huge integration icon wall, floating status widgets over device mockups.

## Pricing / comparison pages
- Not captured.
- Nav has "Pricing" at top level, "Compare Tailscale" (/compare) under Platform, and "Make the case" (a page to help a developer sell Tailscale internally). "Bring Tailscale to Work" also appears in the community menu, which shows a bottom-up adoption strategy.
- Docs: in the top utility bar (DOCS) and under Platform → Docs. "DOWNLOAD" is also a top-level utility link.

## What makes it sell
- The free CTA is emphasised in the nav ("Get started - it's free!") and the homepage shows the install as two terminal commands.
- Quantified customer outcomes with named brands, early.
- The tweet wall gives a genuinely developer-voiced endorsement. Tailscale is famous for word of mouth, and the page leans into it.
- Bottom-up adoption support ("Homelab" product, "Make the case", "Bring Tailscale to Work") turns individual devs into internal champions.
- A calm, warm, unflashy aesthetic signals reliability, which suits security software.

## Weaknesses
- The headline is generic ("best… for the AI era") and the subhead is a long buzzword list (Zero Trust, SASE, PAM). It is less sharp than the product deserves.
- The primary CTA is small and the hero has no visual. The product only appears at the fold.
- The privacy dialog dims the whole first viewport on arrival (seen in the capture).

## Steal for cetra
- Install-to-UI composite: a terminal (install, run) stacked with the real UI it produces (the trace list filling up), to make "minutes to first trace" tangible.
- Three case-study stat cards (big number + one line + "Read case study") before the logo wall.
- A tweet/GitHub-comment carousel under a two-tone heading, once we have real users.
- A "Make the case" page that helps a developer pitch self-hosted cetra to their team or security (data stays on your box).
