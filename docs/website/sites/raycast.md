# Raycast (https://www.raycast.com)

## Hero
- Headline: "Your shortcut to everything."
- Subhead: "A collection of powerful productivity tools all within an extendable launcher. Fast, ergonomic and reliable."
- Primary CTA: "Download for Mac" (light-grey pill with an Apple logo, links to /download/mac). Below it, in small mono text: "macOS Tahoe and Apple Silicon required" and "Install via Homebrew | Download V1". There's no secondary marketing CTA; "Download" repeats in the floating nav pill.
- Visual: a full-bleed abstract artwork of diagonal red light-blades with grainy blue edges on near-black (1 canvas). This is the brand's signature "stripes" motif.

## Structure
1. Floating rounded nav bar, then the hero with the red blades and download CTA.
2. "Take shortcuts, not detours.": a full macOS desktop mock with the Raycast window showing Clipboard History, plus a dock of feature tabs that switch the demo.
3. "It's not about saving time…": a giant 3D keyboard whose keys carry the value props ("Fast. Think in milliseconds", "Ergonomic. Keyboard first", "Reliable. 99.8% crash-free rate").
4. "There's an extension for that.": a carousel of colourful extension cards (Linear, Google Translate, Spotify…) with category tabs and "Browse thousands more".
5. "Meet your new virtual assistant" (AI agent): an AI Chat window running a multi-step tool call, plus Quick AI and AI Extensions cards.
6. "Built for professionals like you.": testimonial switcher (Guillermo Rauch, Marques Brownlee…) with "Favorite feature / Top extension" metadata for each person.
7. "Don't repeat yourself.": Snippets, Quicklinks and Hotkeys bento.
8. "What else can Raycast do?": a long grey list with white highlights, next to a Notes app mock.
9. "Stay in the loop.": Slack (37k members) and X (90k followers) cards, plus a YouTube video row.
10. "Build the perfect tools.": developer section on a blueprint grid with isometric line drawings ("FIG.01…"), covering Cross-platform, Built-in UI, Batteries included and Publish to the Store, with "Read the docs".
11. Closing "Take the short way." over a keyboard (cut off by the 14,000px capture limit).

## Proof
- Named testimonials from famous people (Vercel CEO, MKBHD) with personal details ("I forked the Notion Search extension"), about 60% down the page.
- Community numbers (Slack 37k members, X 90k followers) and a "99.8% crash-free rate" stat on the keyboard.
- "Thousands" of extensions.
- No company logo wall.

## Product shown how
- The real UI appears in the second section, inside a full desktop mock with switchable feature tabs, so it's almost interactive.
- The AI chat shows an actual tool-calling trace (Fetching assigned tasks → Creating Things task).
- Most sections are product UI or product-adjacent art. There are 124 images and no code blocks on the homepage; the developer section uses illustrations instead of code.

## Visual style
- Type: Inter at 64px, weight 600, normal letter-spacing (bold and friendly). Mono for small captions (the system-requirements line, "FIG" labels).
- Colour: near-black rgb(7,8,10) #07080A, signature Raycast red #FF6363 (also the first swatch in the clipboard demo) blended into hot-red #E0263A-ish blades, with secondary blue (#56C2FF) and green (#59D499) accents in extension cards.
- Dark, cinematic, very long (about 16,000px) but airy.
- Signature devices: the red diagonal blades as a recurring texture, a 3D keyboard as an information layout, small two-tone section headers (white bold title plus grey subtitle, at small size), and a blueprint grid with isometric line art for the developer section.

## Pricing / comparison pages
- Not captured. "Pricing" and "Pro" are separate nav items, and "Store" is first in the nav.
- Docs: no top-nav Docs link. The developer section has "Read the docs".

## What makes it sell
- One unmistakable action ("Download for Mac") with honest requirements and a Homebrew alternative right under it, which removes friction for developers.
- A brand art motif so strong that every screenshot is recognisable.
- The "feel" pitch ("It's not about saving time. It's about feeling like you're never wasting it.") sells emotion, not just features.
- Testimonials personalised with each person's favourite feature and top extension feel authentic.
- The ecosystem breadth (thousands of extensions, an AI agent) answers "will it work with my tools?".

## Weaknesses
- The page is extremely long, with big empty dark gaps; the main point is clear early, but the page is padded.
- Section headers are small (about 20px), so the hierarchy is weak once you're past the hero.

## Steal for cetra
- Add a mono install line under the CTA (`brew install …` | `docker run …` | "single static binary, Linux/macOS") with honest requirements, as Raycast does with Homebrew.
- Commit to one recurring brand motif (e.g. a waterfall or flame-graph "span" texture) used as hero art and section dividers.
- Lay out the core values as a physical metaphor, e.g. spec badges on the binary, like Raycast's keyboard keys ("1 binary", "X MB RAM", "Y spans/s").
- Build a developer/extensibility section on a blueprint grid with FIG-numbered line art (OTLP in, SDKs, API, MCP).

## Scores
- Beauty 5/5: iconic red blades, gorgeous dark product rendering.
- Selling 4/5: one clear download CTA and emotional copy, but long.
- Clarity 4/5: "shortcut to everything" plus the launcher demo is quickly clear.
- Proof 3/5: celebrity quotes and community counts, no logos or metrics.
- Product-showing 5/5: real UI with feature switching, starting in section two.
