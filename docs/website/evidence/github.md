# GitHub evidence: pains with LLM observability tools (collected 2026-11-13)

Source: public issues and discussions in langfuse/langfuse, Arize-ai/phoenix, comet-ml/opik, Helicone/helicone, lmnr-ai/lmnr, langwatch/langwatch and mlflow/mlflow (tracing/GenAI only), searched read-only with `gh`. braintrustdata has no public repo with a product issue tracker, so it is not covered.

How to read this:
- Reaction counts are low everywhere. Infra issues almost never get more than 10 👍. "Strength" mostly comes from the same pain showing up in many separate issues and repos, not from a few viral threads.
- Verbatim text: to stay within my copyright limits, each item's quoted text is the **issue title**, which is a short factual label. There is one short body quote in theme A. Everything else is paraphrased.
- Counts are 👍 reactions and comment counts at collection time. "(maintainer/bot-filed)" marks issues that read as filed by maintainers or automated agents, not by end users. They are weaker evidence.

## 3. What surprised me / counter-evidence

- **Infra pain is common but quiet.** The most-reacted issues in each repo since 2025 are about features and SDKs, not deployment. Langfuse's top items are Python 3.14 support (49 👍), media previews, prompt conditionals and Anthropic thinking blocks (~19 👍 each), and its n8n integration discussion has 410 upvotes. Phoenix's top items are prompt files, dataset labels and OTel GenAI semconv. The single loudest infra thread (langfuse#6572) has 10 👍. Infra complaints come from many people with few votes each. They are better evidence of *churn and friction* than of *top-of-mind demand*.
- **Many self-hosters accept the multi-service stack and scale it up.** They don't ask for something simpler. Examples: a 200Gi ClickHouse with ZooKeeper (langfuse#10533), 3×16-vCPU ClickHouse replicas (discussion#19002), questions about EKS disaster recovery and zero-downtime v3→v4 upgrades, and a community member who scripted the whole six-component stack onto one host (langfuse discussion#19477). In the v4 thread, people cheered the *speed* gains and asked when the Helm chart would *include* ClickHouse again. Nobody asked to drop it.
- **The vendor has said no to "simple mode".** Langfuse maintainers turned down a Postgres-only lightweight mode, citing complexity and bugs from running variant setups, and v4 doubles down on ClickHouse. So the gap is real, but the incumbent won't fill it.
- **A simple database does not mean fast.** Phoenix runs as one container on SQLite or Postgres, and it draws the *same* "slow as data grows" complaints (theme D), plus SQLite lock contention (phoenix#6845, phoenix#11010). For a single-binary product, the "fast at volume" claim is the part that has to be proven. Single-node simplicity alone isn't praised as a reason people stay.
- **"All-in-one" images don't fix fragility.** Helicone's all-in-one container (supervisord running ClickHouse, migrations, API and gateway) is behind several of the reports in theme B. Users want a genuinely single-process tool, not many services packed into one container.
- **Weak or absent signals:** air-gapped or on-prem (1 item), pricing or cost of the tool (none; that lives off GitHub), SQLite as an explicit wish (none; the wish is "reuse the DB I already have"), and lock-in or export worries (only indirect, via SQL access and span download requests). GDPR and retention appear, but only as a handful of low-vote items.
- **Watch the source quality.** A noticeable share of 2026 issues are highly structured reports that read like maintainer or AI-agent output. Examples: Phoenix #17600 and #17561, and LangWatch's "ledger" issues with 2,500 comments, which I excluded. I marked maintainer-filed items. They show what maintainers see in production, but they are not independent user voices. Reaction counts in Helicone's 2026 issues may also be skewed by a few frustrated users.
