Alert rules watch a metric of a project over a sliding window and notify you when it crosses a threshold. Rules belong to a project. Members and above manage them.

<Image src={alerts} alt="The alerts page: the project's alert rules." />

## Where to find them

In the sidebar, **Alerts** (under **Monitor**) lists the project's rules. **New rule** opens the rule form. The strip above the list counts the rules by state (**Firing**, **No data**, **OK**, **Disabled**), and selecting a count filters the list. Each row shows the name, state, condition, last value, a trend, when the rule was last evaluated, its channels and an **Enabled** switch. Selecting a row opens the rule beside the list, with **Edit**, **Delete rule** and the rule's **History**. **Recent events** under the list shows the latest events of every rule, with how many of their deliveries were sent.

The rule form names things differently from the fields below: **Metric** (Error rate, Cost, p95 latency, Score, Issue occurrences), **Latency of** (Whole traces, LLM spans), **Aggregation** (Average, Pass rate), **Window**, **Filter**, **Comparator** (Above, At or above, Below, At or below), **Threshold**, **Minimum samples**, **Notifications** (the channels), **Remind while firing** with **Remind every (minutes)**, and **Enabled**. A preview next to the form plots the metric. In the form, the threshold of an error rate or a pass rate is in percent, where the API uses a fraction from 0 to 1.

## What a rule watches

| Metric | What it measures over the window | Extra fields |
|---|---|---|
| `error_rate` | The fraction of traces with at least one error span, from 0 to 1. | `min_count` (default 20) |
| `cost` | The total cost in USD of the traces. It is 0 when there are none. | |
| `latency_p95` | The 95th percentile duration in milliseconds, of traces or of LLM spans (`target`: `trace`, the default, or `llm_span`). | `min_count` (default 20) |
| `score` | The average of the scores with a given name (`score_name`), or the pass rate of boolean scores (`aggregation`: `avg` or `pass_rate`). | `min_count` (default 10) |
| `issue_occurrences` | The number of times an issue happened: the occurrences of one issue, with the issues merged into it, or of every issue of a source except the ignored and merged ones. Zero is a count. | `issue_id` or `source_id`, exactly one |

Every rule has:

- a `window`: `1m`, `5m`, `15m`, `1h`, `6h` or `24h`;
- a `comparator`: `gt`, `gte`, `lt` or `lte`;
- a `threshold`, in the unit of the metric;
- an optional `filter`, a [filter](/docs/reference/) expression over traces. There is no `group_by`: to watch the cost of one model, add a filter for that model.

Evaluation traces and experiment traces are not counted, unless the filter names the `environment` for evaluation traces.

A window with fewer samples than `min_count` has too little data to judge. cetra does not treat it as a breach.

An issue occurrences rule has no filter, no minimum samples and no trace exclusions. Its window must be `15m` or more, because cetra records an occurrence only after its trace completes, and a shorter window can miss it. cetra refuses `1m` and `5m` for this metric.

## Alert on an issue

Use an issue occurrences rule to hear when an issue keeps happening.

1. Open the issue and select **Create alert**. The rule form opens with the metric **Issue occurrences**, the issue, a window of 1 hour, **Above** and a threshold of 0.
2. Change the threshold to the count that matters. With **Above** and 0, the rule fires on the first occurrence in the window.
3. Choose the channels and save the rule.

An error source writes its occurrences within a minute or two of the trace, so a 15 minute or 1 hour window works. For an issue from a bad score, feedback or topic source, use a window of 6 or 24 hours. cetra writes those occurrences when the trace joins an issue, which can be minutes after its score, or in a refit, which runs every 6 hours. Each occurrence is dated at the start of its trace, so a refit can add occurrences up to about 6 hours in the past, and a shorter window can miss them.

A rule on an issue counts that issue and the issues merged into it, including an ignored issue. A rule on a source counts its issues that are not ignored or merged, and the issues merged into them.

## When a rule fires

cetra evaluates each enabled rule once a minute for windows up to 15 minutes, and every five minutes for longer windows. A rule is in one of three states:

- `ok`: the latest reading is not breaching.
- `firing`: the reading breached the threshold. cetra records a `fired` event and notifies each channel.
- `no_data`: a rule that was firing has had too few samples twice in a row. cetra records a `no_data` event and the notification says "No data". It does not resolve the rule, because a silent application is not a healthy one.

A firing rule returns to `ok` after two clear readings in a row, and cetra records a `resolved` event. With `notify_while_firing` on, cetra sends a reminder every `cooldown_s` seconds (300 to 604,800, default 3,600) while the rule keeps firing.

If a query fails, the rule keeps its state and shows the error. A failure is never read as "no data".

Changing a rule's condition, or disabling and re-enabling it, resets it to `ok` without a notification.

## Channels

A channel is a destination defined once per project. Manage them in the project's **Settings**, under **Channels**, with **New channel**. A rule form also links to that page when the project has no channel yet. A rule lists up to 10 channels of its project. A project has at most 100 rules and 20 channels. Channel secrets are stored encrypted, and the API never returns a URL path, a secret or a header value.

### Slack

Create an incoming webhook in your Slack workspace and paste its `https` URL as the channel's URL. cetra posts a message with the rule, project, metric, value, threshold, window, sample count and buttons to the rule and to the matching traces.

### Webhook

cetra sends a `POST` with a JSON body to your URL. A webhook channel has a URL, a secret of 16 to 256 characters, and up to 10 extra headers.

```json
{
  "type": "alert.fired",
  "event_id": "…",
  "rule": { "id": "…", "name": "High error rate", "metric": "error_rate" },
  "project": { "id": "…", "name": "support-bot" },
  "value": 0.083,
  "count": 120,
  "threshold": 0.05,
  "comparator": "gt",
  "window": "5m",
  "window_from": "…",
  "window_to": "…",
  "occurred_at": "…",
  "url": "https://cetra.example.com/<org>/<project>/traces?from=…&to=…&filter=…",
  "rule_url": "https://cetra.example.com/<org>/<project>/alerts/<rule id>"
}
```

`type` is `alert.fired`, `alert.resolved`, `alert.reminder` or `alert.test`. `value` and `count` are `null` when the window had no data. Issue notifications use a different body, `issues.detected`, described on the [Issues page](/docs/evaluation/issues/#notifications).

Each request carries two headers:

- `X-Cetra-Delivery`: the delivery ID. It is the same on every retry of one delivery, so use it to ignore duplicates.
- `X-Cetra-Signature`: `t=<unix seconds>,v1=<hex>`, where the hex is the HMAC-SHA256 of `<t>.<raw body>` with your secret.

Verify the signature on the raw body, before you parse it:

{/* untested: verifies a webhook request */}
```python
import hashlib
import hmac
import time


def verify(secret: str, header: str, body: bytes, tolerance: int = 300) -> bool:
    parts = dict(item.split("=", 1) for item in header.split(","))
    timestamp = parts["t"]
    if abs(time.time() - int(timestamp)) > tolerance:
        return False
    signed = timestamp.encode() + b"." + body
    expected = hmac.new(secret.encode(), signed, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    return hmac.compare_digest(expected, parts["v1"])
```

### Email

An email channel has 1 to 20 recipients, and cetra sends one message per recipient. It needs [SMTP](/docs/self-hosting/email/). Without SMTP the channel shows "SMTP not configured".

When sign-up is `open`, each recipient must be a verified member of the org. Each org can send 1,000 alert emails per UTC day, test sends included.

## Issue notifications

An issue source can send its new and regressed issues to channels of the project. You set the channels on the issue source, not on a rule. See [Issues](/docs/evaluation/issues/#notifications).

A source sends at most one message per channel every 10 minutes. The 10 minutes run from the newest issue event in the message, so two messages from one source are about 9 to 10 minutes apart. A message lists up to 20 issues, newest first, and says how many more there are. When you enable a source, or give it its first channel, cetra starts from then and does not send older issues.

## Delivery and history

cetra sends deliveries in the background, at least once. A timeout, a connection error, a `408`, `429` or `5xx` answer, or an SMTP connection, TLS or authentication error is retried with a growing wait (30 seconds, doubling, up to one hour) for up to 8 attempts. Any other failure fails the delivery at once.

cetra never disables a channel on its own. On the **Channels** page, each channel shows a status: **Delivered**, **Last delivery failed**, **No deliveries**, or **SMTP not configured** for an email channel. Open a channel and select **Send test** to send a sample message right away. A test send is limited to 5 a minute per member and per channel.

A rule's history labels each event **Fired**, **Resolved**, **No data** or **Reminder**, and each delivery **Sent**, **Pending** or **Failed**. cetra keeps alert events for 90 days and finished deliveries for 30 days. Issue messages are kept for 90 days, with their deliveries.

In [restore mode](/docs/self-hosting/configuration-reference/) (`restore_mode = true`), cetra evaluates no rule and sends no delivery.

## Limits by plan

A self-hosted instance has no limit on rules unless you set `entitlements.defaults.max_alert_rules`. On a plan with a limit, creating or re-enabling a rule beyond it fails with `403 plan_limit`. See [Plans and licence](/docs/platform/plans-and-licence/).
